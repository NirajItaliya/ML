Transformers · Attention, step by step

Many heads, one meaning.

The word "mole" gets the same vector whether it's an animal, a unit of chemistry or a spot on your skin. Attention is the step that fixes that. One attention head learns one way that context changes meaning. A multi-head block runs dozens of them side by side and adds up what they find.

01

Why context matters

The first step of a transformer is a lookup. Each token gets an embedding: a long vector whose directions carry meaning. But a lookup has no idea which other words are around it.

Take three phrases: "American shrew mole", "One mole of carbon dioxide" and "Take a biopsy of the mole". Each gives "mole" the same starting vector. A trained model can link different directions in embedding space to the different meanings. Something still has to look at the neighbours and push the vector toward the right one. That is the attention block's job.

🗼

"Eiffel tower"

"tower" alone is generic. With "Eiffel" before it, the vector should move toward Paris, France and things made of iron.

🧸

"miniature Eiffel tower"

Add "miniature" and the vector should move again, away from "large, tall things".

🔎

"…the murderer was"

Feed in most of a mystery novel. The last vector, which started as plain "was", must soak up the whole plot, because only it predicts the next word.

So the goal is to take the initial embeddings $\vec E_1,\dots,\vec E_n$ and turn them into refined embeddings $\vec E'_1,\dots,\vec E'_n$. Each refined vector should carry what its word means here, not what the word means in the dictionary. Information may have to travel across long distances, not just from the word next door.

Mental model: attention moves information between token positions. The MLP block that follows it, the subject of 3b1b's Chapter 7 "How might LLMs store facts", works on each position separately and is where much of the stored knowledge lives. A transformer alternates the two, many times over.
02

One head, one job

The running example is "a fluffy blue creature roamed the verdant forest". We'll pretend one head has a single job: let adjectives update their nouns.

After this head runs, "creature" should end up as something like fluffy, blue creature, and "forest" as verdant forest. Each starting embedding encodes the word and its position. Nothing else is known yet.

Honesty clause (from the video): "adjective → noun" is an illustration. Real heads learn whatever reduces the loss, and their behaviour is "much harder to parse." The example is useful because it makes every matrix easy to name.

One head uses three learned matrices, and each one asks a different question:

MatrixMakesPlain-English role
WQquery $\vec q_i = W_Q\vec E_i$"What am I looking for?" A noun asks: are there adjectives in front of me?
WKkey $\vec k_j = W_K\vec E_j$"What do I offer?" An adjective answers: yes, I'm one.
WVvalue $\vec v_j = W_V\vec E_j$"If I'm relevant, what should I add to you?" For example, the "fluffy" direction.
03

Queries & keys: who is relevant to whom?

The query and key vectors live in a much smaller space than the embedding. In GPT-3 that is 128 dimensions, against 12,288 for the embedding.

$W_Q$ is a $128\times 12{,}288$ matrix. It maps a noun's embedding to a direction in query space that, in our story, means "looking for adjectives in earlier positions." $W_K$ has the same shape. It maps "fluffy" and "blue" to keys that point the same way. To measure how well a key matches a query, take the dot product:

$$ \text{score}(i \leftarrow j) \;=\; \vec q_i\cdot\vec k_j \;=\; (W_Q\vec E_i)^\top (W_K\vec E_j) $$

Do this for every pair and you get an $n\times n$ grid of scores. Where the query of "creature" meets the keys of "fluffy" and "blue", the dot products are large and positive: in the video's language, "fluffy" and "blue" attend to "creature". Where it meets "the", the score is small or negative. The raw scores can be any real number, from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.

Notation flip. 3b1b draws queries as columns and keys as rows, so the grid is $K^\top Q$. The paper, and the widgets on this page, put tokens in rows: the grid is $QK^\top$, where row $i$ is query $i$. It's the same numbers, mirrored along the diagonal.
04

Softmax, $\sqrt{d_k}$, and the attention pattern

Raw scores aren't weights yet. We want each query's weights to be between 0 and 1 and to add up to 1, so we pass each query's scores through softmax.

$$ \alpha_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\exp\!\big(\vec q_i\cdot\vec k_j/\sqrt{d_k}\big)}{\sum_{j'}\exp\!\big(\vec q_i\cdot\vec k_{j'}/\sqrt{d_k}\big)} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \text{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big)V $$

The resulting grid $\alpha$ is called the attention pattern. Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$? If the entries of $\vec q$ and $\vec k$ are roughly independent with variance 1, their dot product is a sum of $d_k$ terms and has variance about $d_k$. With $d_k=128$ the scores would typically be around $\pm 11$. Softmax would then put almost all the weight on a single key, and its gradients would vanish. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ brings the variance back to about 1. The video describes this as being "for numerical stability".

05

Masking, and why context size is expensive

A GPT model is trained to predict the next token at every position at once. One sentence therefore gives many training examples: predict "fluffy" from "a", "blue" from "a fluffy", and so on.

For that to be fair, a word must never see the words after it. Otherwise it could just read the answer. So before the softmax, every score where a later token would influence an earlier one is set to $-\infty$. After the softmax these become exactly 0, and each row still sums to 1. This is masking. In the explorer above, the hatched cells are the masked ones. Untick "causal mask" and the pattern can look ahead.

$$ M_{ij}=\begin{cases}0 & j\le i\\ -\infty & j>i\end{cases}\qquad \alpha=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\Big) $$

The pattern has one row and one column per token, so its size is the square of the context length. GPT-3's context is 2,048 tokens, so each head in each layer builds a $2048\times2048 = 4{,}194{,}304$-entry pattern. GPT-3 has 96 heads × 96 layers = 9,216 heads, so one forward pass computes about 38.7 billion attention weights. This quadratic cost is why making context windows longer is hard. Variants such as chunked or limited-context attention, like the streaming Conformer in the 3D STT explorer, exist mainly to cut that $n^2$.

06

Values: actually moving meaning

The pattern says who is relevant. The value matrix says what to add.

Multiply each word's embedding by the value matrix to get its value vector, $\vec v_j$. For "fluffy", that is the nudge that turns "creature" into "fluffy creature". Then, for each query $i$, weight every value vector by the attention pattern, add them up, and add the result to the original embedding:

$$ \Delta\vec E_i=\sum_{j}\alpha_{ij}\,\vec v_j,\qquad \vec E'_i=\vec E_i+\Delta\vec E_i $$

For "creature", the weights on "fluffy" and "blue" are large and every other weight is close to 0. So "creature" receives mostly the "fluffy" and "blue" value vectors.

The low-rank trick: value-down, then value-up

The value vector has to live in the 12,288-dimensional embedding space, because it is added to an embedding. A full square $W_V$ would have $12{,}288^2 = 150{,}994{,}944$ parameters, about 96 times more than $W_Q$ or $W_K$. Instead the value map is split into two pieces:

$$ W_V \;=\; \underbrace{W_{V\uparrow}}_{12{,}288\times128}\;\underbrace{W_{V\downarrow}}_{128\times12{,}288} \qquad\text{(a rank-128 map)}$$

Value-down squeezes the embedding into the same 128-dimensional space as the queries and keys. Value-up maps the result back out to the embedding space. Each piece has 1,572,864 parameters, the same as $W_Q$ and $W_K$. So one head has four equally sized matrices, about 6.3 million parameters in total.

Naming in papers: "value-down / value-up" is 3b1b's own wording. In the paper and in code, the value matrix of a head means only the value-down part. The value-up matrices of all the heads are stacked into a single output matrix $W_O$, which belongs to the whole block. The next section explains why that stacking is exact.
07

Multi-head attention

Adjectives updating nouns is one way context changes meaning. There are many others, so the block runs many heads in parallel, each with its own $W_Q, W_K, W_{V\downarrow}, W_{V\uparrow}$.

More examples from the video: "they crashed the" before "car" should change the car's shape and condition. "Harry" near "wizard" points to Harry Potter, while "Harry" near "Queen", "Sussex" and "William" points to Prince Harry. Each kind of update needs its own pattern, so different query and key matrices, and its own nudge, so different value matrices. GPT-3 uses 96 heads in every block. Every head proposes its own change for every position, and the block adds them all to the embedding:

$$ \vec E'_i \;=\; \vec E_i+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Delta\vec E_i^{(h)} \;=\; \vec E_i+\sum_{h=1}^{H} W_{V\uparrow}^{(h)}\sum_j \alpha^{(h)}_{ij}\,W_{V\downarrow}^{(h)}\vec E_j $$

The paper writes the same thing as concatenate, then project:

$$ \operatorname{MultiHead}(X)=\big[\,\text{head}_1\;\|\;\cdots\;\|\;\text{head}_H\,\big]\,W_O $$ $$ \text{head}_h=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\tfrac{XW_Q^{(h)}(XW_K^{(h)})^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\Big)XW_{V}^{(h)} $$

These two forms are equal. Cut $W_O$ (size $Hd_k\times d$) into $H$ horizontal slices of $d_k$ rows each. Multiplying the concatenated head outputs by $W_O$ is the same as multiplying each head's output by its own slice and adding the results. Each slice is exactly that head's value-up matrix.

Four heads, one sentence

Each thumbnail is one head's attention pattern on the same sentence. Click a thumbnail to load it into the explorer in §04. The table shows what each head adds to the selected word.
Why not one huge head? One softmax gives each word one set of weights, a single way of deciding what matters. If one head had to track adjectives, the previous word and the subject of a verb all at once, it would have to blur them together. With $H$ heads, each one picks its own pattern, and it costs nothing extra: $H$ heads of size $d_k = d/H$ have exactly as many parameters as one head of size $d$.

Counting parameters

Every attention matrix is $d_k\times d$, and each head has four of them. That gives a simple formula.

$$ \underbrace{4\,d\,d_k}_{\text{per head}}\;\times\;H\;=\;4\,d\,(H d_k)\;=\;\boxed{4d^2}\ \text{per layer}\qquad(\text{when }Hd_k=d) $$

Self-attention vs cross-attention

Everything above is self-attention: the queries, keys and values all come from the same sequence. Cross-attention is almost identical. The one difference is that queries come from one sequence and keys and values from another.

Self-attentionCross-attention
Q comes fromsequence Xsequence X (e.g. the target language, or the text decoder)
K, V come fromsequence Xsequence Y (e.g. the source language, or audio encoder frames)
Pattern shape$n_X\times n_X$$n_X\times n_Y$
Maskingcausal in GPT-style decodersusually none: there is no "later" token to hide
Where you've seen itGPT, BERT, Conformer encodersmachine translation, Whisper's decoder looking at the audio

12

Code it: multi-head attention from scratch

This is the "concatenate, then project" form in PyTorch. Each of $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ holds all $H$ heads in one $d\times d$ matrix, and a reshape separates them.

import math, torch, torch.nn as nn

class MultiHeadAttention(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_model=512, n_heads=8, causal=True):
        super().__init__()
        assert d_model % n_heads == 0
        self.h, self.dk, self.causal = n_heads, d_model // n_heads, causal
        self.W_q = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)  # all heads' W_Q stacked
        self.W_k = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)
        self.W_v = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)  # all heads' "value-down"
        self.W_o = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)  # all heads' "value-up", stacked

    def forward(self, x, kv=None):            # x: (B, n, d); kv given → cross-attention
        B, n, d = x.shape
        src = x if kv is None else kv
        split = lambda t: t.view(B, -1, self.h, self.dk).transpose(1, 2)  # (B, H, n, dk)
        q, k, v = split(self.W_q(x)), split(self.W_k(src)), split(self.W_v(src))

        scores = q @ k.transpose(-2, -1) / math.sqrt(self.dk)   # (B, H, n, m)
        if self.causal and kv is None:
            mask = torch.triu(torch.ones(n, n, dtype=torch.bool, device=x.device), 1)
            scores = scores.masked_fill(mask, float('-inf'))  # later → earlier blocked
        attn = scores.softmax(dim=-1)                    # the attention pattern

        heads = attn @ v                                # (B, H, n, dk)
        concat = heads.transpose(1, 2).reshape(B, n, d)   # [head_1 ‖ … ‖ head_H]
        return x + self.W_o(concat)                      # E' = E + ΔE

mha = MultiHeadAttention(512, 8)
print(sum(p.numel() for p in mha.parameters()))     # 1,048,576 = 4 · 512²
In practice you'd call torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, is_causal=True). It computes the same result with fused, memory-efficient kernels such as FlashAttention, which never store the full $n\times n$ pattern. Real blocks also put a LayerNorm in front, and the residual add usually sits outside the attention module.
13

Recap

  • Goal: turn context-free embeddings $\vec E$ into context-aware ones, $\vec E'=\vec E+\Delta\vec E$.
  • Query · key dot products, divided by $\sqrt{d_k}$, give scores. A causal mask hides future tokens, and softmax turns each row into weights: the attention pattern.
  • Values (value-down, then value-up) say what to add. Each position adds the weighted sum of the value vectors.
  • Multi-head: $H$ heads run in parallel, each with its own pattern, and their outputs are added. That's the same as concatenating them and multiplying by $W_O$.
  • Cost: $4d^2$ parameters per layer (about 58 B over GPT-3's 96 layers), and $n^2$ pattern entries per head. Context length is the expensive part.
  • Why it won: every head and every position can be computed at the same time. That parallelism is what lets attention scale on GPUs.
