01

Understand sound as numbers

Sampling rate, amplitude, duration and channels

At 16 kHz, one second contains 16,000 amplitude samples. A waveform shows pressure change over time, not words or frequencies.

samples = duration_seconds × sample_rate
Completion gate and material

02

Frequency and Fourier intuition

Move from time-only to frequency strength

The Fourier transform asks how much of each frequency exists. A whole-audio FFT loses when those frequencies occurred.

time samples → Fourier transform → frequency strengths
Completion gate and reference

Explain time domain versus frequency domain and identify the frequency peak of a sine wave.

03

Build a spectrogram with STFT

Framing, windowing, FFT and stacking

Apply a windowed FFT to overlapping short frames. Stack spectra so x=time, y=frequency and colour=strength.

X[m,k] = Σ x[n+mH] w[n] exp(-j2πkn/N)
Notebook and expected observations

Changing hop length changes time columns; changing n_fft changes frequency-bin count. The notebook verifies a manual STFT against Librosa.

04

Understand log-Mel features

Perceptual frequency pooling and compression

Mel filters pool STFT power into speech-friendly bands. Log compression reduces dynamic range. One Mel frame is a continuous vector.

Mel power = M · STFT power · encoder shape = (B,T,80)
Notebook and expected observations

Manual triangular filters are checked against Librosa. Compare 40, 80 and 128 bands and inspect the low-frequency spacing.

05

Learn convolution for speech

Local weighted patterns, kernels and channels

Convolution reuses a small learned kernel at every location. Speech front ends often use 2D time-frequency convolution; Conformer uses 1D temporal convolution.

Conv2d input: (B,C,T,F) · Conv1d input: (B,C,T)
Notebook and checks

Run smoothing, time-change and frequency-change kernels. Explain kernel size versus stride and input versus output channels.

06

CNN subsampling

Reduce time and attention cost

Two stride-2 convolutions reduce time and frequency while increasing learned channels. Flatten channel × frequency, never time.

(B,1,300,80) → (B,64,75,20) → (B,75,1280) → (B,75,256)
Notebook and checks

Trace every shape, update variable lengths and verify that 4× shorter time gives about a 16× smaller attention matrix.

07

Send speech features into an encoder

Projection, position and padding mask

CNN features become `(B,T′,D)`, the same structural shape as text encoder input. Relative time position is useful for variable-length utterances.

speech: log-Mel → CNN → flatten → Linear(CF′,D) → position + mask
Prerequisites and checks

Output, alignment and decoding

09

Contextual acoustic output

Understand exactly what H[t] represents

`H[t]` contains current, neighboring and global acoustic evidence. It is a D-dimensional latent vector, not a character prediction.

H = Conformer(X,M) ∈ R^(B×T′×D)
Notebook and visual

Perturb one local frame and observe changes at multiple contextual positions. Compare with BERT's token-aligned rows.

10

Vocabulary logits and probabilities

Map D latent features to V symbols

One shared `Linear(D,V)` head produces frame-level logits. Softmax runs across vocabulary, not time. Argmax is still a frame path.

Z = H W_vocabᵀ + b · (B,T′,D) → (B,T′,V)
Math, notebook and visual

Calculate a four-symbol softmax by hand, verify the shared matrix, and compare correct versus incorrect softmax axes.

11

Greedy CTC decoding

Resolve many acoustic positions into fewer symbols

Argmax each valid frame, merge adjacent repeats, then remove blank. This order preserves repeated transcript letters.

_ h h _ e _ l _ l l _ o _ → hello
Notebook and reference

Enumerate many paths for one transcript, compare greedy path and transcript probability, and prove why valid lengths matter.

12

Know the decoder families

CTC, RNN-T, TDT and attention decoder

CTC consumes frame logits. Transducers combine `H[t]` with previous-token history. AED cross-attends to `H` while generating tokens.

H ├ CTC head · ├ predictor+joiner · └ causal decoder cross-attention
Completion gate and gap

Name each family and whether it uses previous text tokens. Learn RNN-T before TDT because TDT adds a duration distribution to the transducer.

What not to claim

Architecture experiments can prove shapes and data flow. They cannot prove transcription quality.

random encoder + random head = random symbol probabilities
